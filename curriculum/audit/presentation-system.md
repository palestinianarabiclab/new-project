# Tajweed Live-Teaching Presentation System

## Purpose

The Tajweed curriculum is delivered by a teacher during a live, screen-shared
lesson. The interface supports explanation, prediction, oral questioning, and
teacher-observed recitation. It is not a replacement for a qualified teacher or
a self-study copy of the source books.

The course outline files retain the source-page mapping for every unit. Content
must not move between parts merely to make the interface sequence look simpler.

## Required live-lesson sequence

Every lesson uses seven presentation stages:

1. Definition — one learner-friendly meaning before details.
2. Letters, markers, or key condition — selected according to lesson type.
3. Worked example — complete context, target, controlling clue, and reason.
4. Pronunciation or application — teacher model, correct action, common error.
5. Questions — identify, explain, and predict before revealing the answer.
6. Application — guided example followed by an unseen example.
7. Summary — memory formula, final oral application, and homework.

## Lesson-type adaptation

### Letter and rule lessons

Use letters, target/trigger analysis, correct sound, and contrasting errors.
Examples: izhar, idgham, ikhfa, meem saakinah, lam, mudood, qalqalah,
characteristics, tafkheem and tarqeeq.

### Articulation lessons

Replace “trigger letter” with articulation point. Display anatomical grouping,
sound production, teacher modelling, and contrast between neighbouring points.

### Stop/start and written-form lessons

Replace “trigger letter” with marker or reading state. Always identify whether
the learner is connecting, stopping, starting, or making sakt before naming the
decision.

### Orientation, manners, and grammar-term lessons

Replace the letters stage with the controlling idea or category. Application may
be explanation and decision-making rather than sound production.

## Screen-sharing rules

- Show one stage at a time.
- Keep teacher notes and source details outside the shared lesson layer.
- Reveal the reason only after the learner predicts.
- Display Qur'anic examples in complete usable context.
- Use consistent visual roles: blue target, orange controlling clue, green
  correct application, red common error.
- A visual quiz never certifies recitation mastery.
- Mastery requires teacher-observed performance in a new example.

## Verification policy

- Source alignment and page mapping are structural checks.
- Qur'anic transcription must be checked against the source and mushaf.
- Articulation, timing, ghunnah, madd, qalqalah, and transmitted Hafs forms
  require review by a qualified Tajweed teacher.
- Items marked `teacher-review-required` remain visibly unresolved until that
  review is completed.
